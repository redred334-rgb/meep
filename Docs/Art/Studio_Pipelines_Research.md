# How Gearbox (Borderlands) and Telltale (The Walking Dead) make their inked / comic look

Research date: 2026-10-09. Sources were reached through search-result excerpts only (full-page fetch was blocked), so quotes are short and page-level. Items are tagged **[DEV]** (developer / studio artist statement), **[PRESS]** (journalist relaying a studio statement), **[ARTIST]** (outside artist breakdown or tutorial), **[FAN]** (community observation / speculation).

## Summary

- Borderlands is **not a cel shader**. Gearbox's own description, repeated from 2009 to 2025, is hand-painted textures with the ink work drawn *into* the diffuse / base color, plus one procedural step: a Sobel edge-detection post-process that adds the black outline around silhouettes. Hatching, shadows, highlights and interior lines are all hand-painted; "no procedural solution would provide the same quality of line work."
- Telltale (pre-2016 engine) is a **painterly / watercolor** look: Lambert diffuse + Blinn-Phong specular, with most lighting and even specular highlights **painted into the diffuse**, little or no normal mapping, and linework stored on a **separate texture** so the shader can kill specular on the lines. The 2016 PBS rewrite added a banded ("cell banding") diffuse model and improved mesh outlines.
- Practical difference: Borderlands = heavy, hierarchical ink + hatching in the texture, large clean color fields, screen-space Sobel outline; Telltale = soft painted lighting in the diffuse, thin separate line map, softer banding.

Unconfirmed from this research (could not reach the slides): what buffers the Gearbox Sobel pass reads (depth vs. normals), its ~3 ms budget, a stencil mask excluding foliage, and the exact line thickness. Those are plausible but unverified; see the Gearbox section.

## Gearbox / Borderlands

### Confirmed (developer or studio statements)

- The original 2009 description from Gearbox: "The new technique uses hand-drawn textures, scanned in and colored in Photoshop", and then software "adds graphic-novel-style outlines, sharpens shadows, and draws lines on hills and inclines". Character models were also reworked with exaggerated proportions. [PRESS relaying Gearbox] [Destructoid](https://www.destructoid.com/borderlands-explains-its-not-cel-shaded-actually-art-style/)
- Gearbox called the look "concept art style", not cel-shading; the goal was "a game that looked like its concept art" (Pitchford, E3 2009). [PRESS] [Destructoid E3 09](https://www.destructoid.com/e3-09-how-the-borderlands-art-style-was-an-act-of-madness/), [Wikipedia: Claptrap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claptrap)
- Brian Martel (CGW, Nov 2009): the outline "was introduced in the texture so there is an inked look to it that sits on top of what looks like a gouache painting"; look = "a dark outline around each asset, while inside, inked markings sit atop hand-painted textures"; artists painted directly on models in Photoshop "when we needed to highlight the edges in the new style"; engine was a heavily modified UE3. [DEV] [CGW "A Fine Line"](https://www.cgw.com/Publications/CGW/2009/Volume-32-Issue-11-Nov-2009-/A-Fine-Line.aspx)
- SIGGRAPH 2010 "Making Concept Art Real for Borderlands" (Aaron Thibault, Sean "Zoner" Cavanaugh, Senior Rendering Engineer) in the "Stylized Rendering in Games" course; a contemporary review says it covered "the development change from photorealistic to stylistic rendering and art (and the code/artist balance), and the sobel edge filter they used." [DEV talk, secondhand summary] [Course page](https://stylized.realtimerendering.com/), [Adrian Boeing overview](http://adrianboeing.blogspot.com/2010/09/siggraph-2010-course-papers-overview.html)
- GDC 2010 "Borderlands and the 11th Hour Art Style Change (Kids, Don't Try This at Home)": Martel built a secret prototype "in a closet" after the "Truth Team" found the game too close to Fallout 3; the change came ~75% through production; Mikey Neumann presented in Pitchford's place; a small team built "style sheets" of conceptual terms with examples from other media; Martel paired a comic-book artist with the lead character designer. [DEV talk via press] [GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/behind-borderlands-11th-hour-style-change/1100-6253257/), [Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/b/news/archive/2010/03/13/borderlands-and-the-11th-hour-art-style-change.aspx), [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1012480/Borderlands-and-the-11th-Hour)
- Gearbox art team on Polycount (BL2 art mega thread): "from there it is mostly hand painting details, shadows, highlights, inks, etc... the final look you see in game is all done in the textures"; "the shaders actually don't do anything all that special." [DEV] [Polycount BL2 mega thread p3](https://polycount.com/discussion/109497/borderlands-2-art-mega-thread-omg-image-heavy/p3)
- Scott Kester (BL3 art director): "The only thing that is procedural in Borderlands is our outline treatment, a 'sobel' edge detection that adds the black outline around the characters and environment." The team "also tried a 'hatched based' shading method that actually added 'hand ink' shadows to the scenes" (a motion-comic experiment). [DEV] [Cook & Becker](https://www.cookandbecker.com/en/article/224/borderlands-3-art-director-scott-kester-on-communicating-an-attitude.html)
- Kester: "all that ink is hand done, none of that is procedural, every bit of it is me and a bunch of the other guys"; BL3 "went a little too crazy initially, but brought it back." [DEV] [TheSixthAxis](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2019/05/01/borderlands-3-interview-bosses-promethea-4k-hdr/)
- Kester: some BL3 experiments "went too far"; "certain shadows and hatchings were all hand drawn and you can see them in the intro illustrations." [DEV] [Red Bull](https://www.redbull.com/za-en/Borderlands-3-Scott-Kester-interview)
- Gearbox BL3 Art Blast: "every single texture has hand illustrated elements, as there is no procedural solution that would provide the same quality of line work"; BL3 added PBR. [DEV] [CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/2019/10/discover-how-borderlands-3s-unique-art-style-was-created/), [ArtStation Magazine](https://magazine.artstation.com/2019/09/gearbox-software-borderlands-3-art-blast/)
- Romain Lambert (Gearbox Quebec environment artist): "Borderlands is not a game completely made in cell shading, the biggest job is done by hand in the textures... Each artist will create their texture with an inking pass on top, and after that, shaders and post process will refine everything." [DEV] [exp-points Q&A](https://www.exp-points.com/borderlands-3-qa)
- Lambert's own Substance Painter breakdown (follows "the techniques and rules that we have at Gearbox"): ink **first**, before materials, so the lines are not biased by generators/grunge; inks painted by hand into **Base Color and also into Roughness**; line style differs between plastic and metal; AO in Multiply and Curvature in Overlay on top of the stack for extra shadow/highlight; **white highlight lines and shadows painted only in Base Color at ~25% opacity**; dirt/damage via generators then grunges; height details (bolts) via alphas. [DEV] [80.lv Compact Gun](https://80.lv/articles/making-a-compact-gun-in-the-borderlands-style)
- Borderlands 4 (Adam May, art director): UE5, "hand crafted inks" + Nanite/Lumen; "dynamic inking" for foliage: a noise map groups foliage into clumps and "we ink the outside of that" instead of every leaf, to avoid "a wall of inky noise". [DEV] [COGconnected](https://cogconnected.com/feature/borderlands-4s-adam-may/), [CGMagazine](https://www.cgmagonline.com/interviews/borderlands-4-adam-may/), [BL4 Art Blast](https://magazine.artstation.com/2025/11/gearbox-software-borderlands-4-art-blast/)
- BL3 config exposes `EdgeDetectionTexelOffset` (default -1.0; 1.25-1.99 thins the outline), confirming a screen-space texel-offset edge pass; earlier games let players swap the scene post-process class to remove outlines. [FAN report of shipped config] [Gearbox forums](https://forums.gearboxsoftware.com/t/config-settings-for-outlines-cell-shading-and-hdr/3128406)

### Secondary / artist tutorials (not Gearbox staff unless noted)

- methodj.com series (Photoshop/Maya/Mudbox): start from photo ref, Cutout filter was too noisy so hand-paint to sharpen shapes and add value contrast; make a **crevice map from the high-res sculpt** (low points dark, high points bright); structural lines thicker/heavier, cross-hatch thinner/lighter, on **separate layers**; build heavy blacks by stacking lines and taper hatching by erasing; cross-hatch mainly **under wrinkles / in shadow**; shadows later as a soft low-opacity airbrush layer, **Soft Light ~60%**, only where there are large wrinkles (small wrinkles already read via hatching); highlights = white on raised points (wrinkle peaks, knee pressing fabric); coffee-stain / ink-splatter overlays; "big open spaces of color to contrast against the drawn-on lines." [ARTIST] [page 1](https://www.methodj.com/borderlands-style-texture-creation/), [page 2](https://www.methodj.com/borderlands-style-texture-creation/2/), [page 3](https://www.methodj.com/borderlands-style-texture-creation/3/)
- 7he1ndigo DeviantArt tutorial: 1024 canvas, #808080 base; ink on a layer above with a hard round brush, black, pen pressure; cross-hatch deep areas, quick lines for surface scratches; **outlines and most important cracks thickest, supporting linework much thinner**; highlights with the same brush on a layer *below* the ink in a much lighter value on raised points; a lighter-grey accent layer under that; a darker shadow layer under that for deep areas; texture/material last. [ARTIST] [DeviantArt](https://www.deviantart.com/7he1ndigo/art/How-to-Create-Textures-for-Borderlands-556725103)
- Polycount "The Borderlands workflow?": start by outlining the major UV-shell edges, then secondary edges, then invented lines; straight UVs help; recurring motifs: quilted metal, edge scrapes, little circles, **hatched lines for recessed surfaces**; "the less important the line, the thinner it should be"; highlights separate from lighting, round pen-pressure brush, **Soft Light 50-75%**; "you can't have a highlight and an ink line in the exact same spot, so decide which is more important"; heavy inking hides normal-map detail (commenters disagree on how much BL2 used normal maps). [ARTIST/FAN] [Polycount](https://polycount.com/discussion/135111/the-borderlands-workflow)
- Matthew Messner (80.lv, Porta Potty): all texturing in Substance Painter on one 4k map; paintover layers emphasise edges/shadows; shadow = darker fill in **Multiply**, highlight = lighter fill in **Lighten**; edge highlight on dark plastic driven by baked **curvature**; inked by hand "same process as comic inking", referencing Gearbox Art Blast line/splatter styles; UE4 toon outline post-process from an Epic tutorial. [ARTIST] [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/making-a-cel-shaded-porta-potty-in-the-borderlands-style)
- Michael Cox (80.lv, Laser Rifle): inking first; tried curvature-as-mask then settled on **two black fill layers masked by the UV Border Distance generator** (primary edge line + offset line) plus a hand-painted line layer; curvature Overlay 20% to pop. [ARTIST] [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/creating-a-laser-rifle-in-the-borderlands-style)
- GennaroDima (The Rookies): curvature generator as a *baseline* for black lines, then one brush layer to erase unwanted lines and another to add thinner ones; curvature also for edge highlights. [ARTIST] [The Rookies](https://www.therookies.co/projects/68668)
- Fan reconstructions of the outline pass sample depth (and sometimes color/normals) with a Sobel kernel (8 weighted neighbours; ~10 taps for depth+color). These are guesses, not Gearbox's implementation. [FAN] [Godot Shaders](https://godotshaders.com/shader/3d-edge-detection-shader-borderlands-style/), [simonschreibt comment](https://simonschreibt.de/gat/cell-shading/)

## Telltale / The Walking Dead

### Confirmed (developer statements)

- Pre-PBS Telltale Tool: "lambertian diffuse model with blinn-phong as specular (where applicable for characters)"; art "heavily hand-painted"; "most environment assets had no normal maps, and most characters had painted lighting"; environment lighting baked via Maya/mental ray; characters had up to 4 dynamic lights but "specularity was rarely used and art often opted to paint highlights in heavily." [DEV] [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/production/telltale-s-move-to-pbs-shifting-technologies-and-practices)
- "Hand painted diffuse maps on characters typically included heavily painted lighting information, such as sharp and hot specular highlights on noses and lips. Linework was generally stored on a separate texture so the shader could nullify specular correctly." [DEV] [tightenupthegraphics repost](https://tightenupthegraphics.com/2017/11/28/telltales-move-to-pbs-shifting-technologies-and-practices/)
- The 2016 (Batman) rewrite added "an entirely new diffuse model that supported advanced cell banding and color blending", "more advanced mesh outlines", GGX specular, volumetric fog; problems included a zombie whose mis-painted specular albedo left a blue skin hue once lighting went dynamic, and tripled texture memory (fixed by merging assets into one material via Simplygon); a Maya shader-preview plugin and color-ID maps generated from the diffuse layer setup helped artists. [DEV] same two links.
- Telltale texture-artist postings: "hand-painted textures from scratch to finish", "excellent traditional painting skills a must", Photoshop + Maya, "Illustrative preferred"; spec/normal maps only a plus. [DEV job post] [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/art/get-a-job-be-a-texture-artist-at-telltale-games-2)
- Ryan Benno (Telltale env artist): worked with tech art "to develop the shadow and inking style for many environments and characters in The Wolf Among Us" and visual targets for lighting/texturing. [DEV] [ArtStation](https://ryanbenno.artstation.com/projects/rRDYq5)

## How they differ

- Line source: Borderlands = hand-inked lines in the base color (plus roughness) + Sobel outline post. Telltale = thinner line work on a **separate line texture**, with mesh outlines from the engine; no screen-space Sobel mentioned.
- Shading: Borderlands paints hatching, Soft Light/Multiply shadows and ~25% white highlights into the texture on top of normal lighting (PBR since BL3). Telltale paints *lighting itself* (including hot speculars) into the diffuse under a plain Lambert; post-2016 a banded diffuse does the stylisation.
- Density: Borderlands = heavy ink hierarchy + hatching in recesses, but large clean color fields. Telltale = soft watercolor gradients, minimal hatching, cleaner skin.
- Normals: Telltale mostly none pre-2016. Borderlands uses them, but artists say heavy inking flattens them.

## Implications for our procedural painter

1. Paint into the texture: interior ink (seams, panel lines, cracks, wrinkles), hatching in recesses, soft shadow and highlight passes, grime/splatter. Leave to the engine: the **silhouette outline** (screen-space Sobel on depth/normals, texel-offset tunable), lighting, and PBR response. Do not paint silhouette-tracing black into the texture.
2. Ink first, before materials and before grunge, so lines are not biased by generators. Ink goes into base color; optionally copy it into roughness so lines stay matte.
3. Line hierarchy: outlines of UV-shell / major form edges thickest; important cracks next; supporting detail much thinner; "the less important the line, the thinner it should be." Keep structural lines and hatching on separate layers.
4. Line sources: curvature/cavity (crevice map from the sculpt) and UV-border-distance are acceptable *baselines*, but every real pipeline then erases unwanted lines and adds hand-style thin ones. Add jitter/taper; build heavy blacks by stacking strokes, taper hatching by erasing its ends.
5. Hatching rules: only in recessed / shadow areas (under wrinkles, inside panels), thinner and lighter than structural lines, directional cross-hatch; small wrinkles get hatching instead of a painted shadow.
6. Shadow painting: a soft low-opacity airbrush layer, Multiply or Soft Light at ~50-60%, darker variant of the fill color, only where forms are large; do not shade everywhere.
7. Highlight painting: white/lighter fill on raised points (edges, wrinkle peaks), Soft Light 50-75% or Lighten, or plain white at ~25% opacity; never place a highlight on the same pixel as an ink line (ink wins).
8. Keep large open color fields; the look depends on clean flat areas contrasting with the lines. Cap total ink coverage per material.
9. Material-specific line style (plastic vs metal vs cloth) and motif library: quilted metal, edge scrapes, small circles, scratch ticks, splatter overlays.
10. Separate line texture: optional. Telltale's reason was to zero specular under lines with a Lambert/Blinn-Phong model; Borderlands bakes ink into base color and roughness instead. For a PBR engine, bake ink into base color + roughness (Gearbox) and only keep a separate line mask if you need to animate or scale line weight at runtime.
11. Foliage / dense instancing: ink clump silhouettes (noise-grouped), not every leaf, to avoid "a wall of inky noise" (BL4).
