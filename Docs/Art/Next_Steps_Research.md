## 1. Borderlands-style hair, beard and brow assets

### 1.1 How Gearbox builds it (what the sources actually say)
- Official Art Blasts exist for BL3 (2019) and BL4 (Nov 2025) on ArtStation Magazine, but they are credit galleries, not technical hair write-ups: https://magazine.artstation.com/2019/09/gearbox-software-borderlands-3-art-blast/ , https://magazine.artstation.com/2025/11/gearbox-software-borderlands-4-art-blast/ . Individual BL3 character posts (e.g. Zane, "perfectly disheveled hair") show the result but not the method: https://www.artstation.com/artwork/dOB3le .
- Style analyses agree the base geometry is deliberately simple and the character detail lives in hand-drawn, scanned textures with outlines and hardened shadows added in the shader: https://vsquad.art/blog/what-art-style-is-borderlands-exploring-the-borderlands-art-style , https://retrostylegames.com/blog/game-art-design-like-borderlands-style/ .
- Polycount's Borderlands-emulation thread notes two things artists miss: the shadowing is helped by normal maps (not diffuse-only), and the contrast/shading provides natural boundaries to lay ink lines on: https://polycount.com/discussion/135461/borderlands-inspired-character .
- General game-hair reference (why cards are a pain): alpha-blended cards need soft edges but cause sorting problems and are often shaded unlit/emissive: http://wiki.polycount.com/wiki/HairTechnique . AAA card/beard workflow for comparison: https://80.lv/articles/making-hair-and-beards-for-aaa-games .
- **(unverified, our observation)** No source documents "BL hair = solid sculpted clumps + painted ink separations", but that matches the look and the "simple geometry, detail in texture" statements above. Treat it as our working assumption: opaque chunky clump meshes, with strand separation and shadow drawn into the albedo and reinforced by a normal map, outlines from the post-process/shader. Beards in that style are mostly texture on the face (stubble, short beards) and small opaque meshes for long/pointy beards; brows are painted into the face texture in the games. We saw no evidence of alpha-card hair in Borderlands.

### 1.2 Practical Tripo -> Blender -> UE pipeline for hair pieces
- **Tripo prompts that work**: Tripo's own gallery uses prompts like "Generate a standalone 3D hair mesh only, no head, face, or body … low-poly but volumetric, simple shapes, centered at world origin … discard any head" and a kawaii example that enumerates exclusions (no character/face/eyes/mouth/body/skin): https://studio.tripo3d.ai/3d-model/generate-a-standalone-3d-hair-mesh-only-no-head-face-or-body-styli-1b667c9f-7fa0-4a66-9b5d-a2781cf50c8a , https://studio.tripo3d.ai/3d-model/generate-a-standalone-3d-hair-model-only-no-character-no-face-no-ey-e8b2aa8f-898b-4b8e-b0ed-1f7310a0c98f . Tripo's prompt guide: name the hairstyle, parting and flow ("wind-blown"), avoid generic "detailed hair"; include style indicator and quality cues: https://www.tripo3d.ai/blog/explore/ai-3d-model-generator-generating-cloth-and-hair-geometry , https://www.tripo3d.ai/blog/text-to-3d-prompt-engineering .
- **Common failures**: thin structures fuse into noise; a head/scalp gets generated and must be cut away; scale is arbitrary and must be checked against the base mesh: https://www.tripo3d.ai/blog/explore/ai-3d-model-generator-and-cables-chains-hair-generation-challenges , https://www.tripo3d.ai/blog/explore/ai-3d-model-generator-converting-hair-cards-for-games . Treat output as a volumetric blockout and retopo/remesh (Tripo Smart Mesh caps quads at 25k faces, tris at 50k): https://www.tripo3d.ai/features/smart-mesh .
- **Triangle budget**: no source gives a hair-only budget. Mobile hero characters are quoted at ~1.5k-5k tris total, PC/console 15k-50k: https://blog.neural4d.com/user-guide/polygon-count-for-3d-game-assets-printing-and-webar/ , https://www.messypoly.com/learn/polygon-budgets-mobile-games . Opaque meshes are cheaper than layered transparent ones regardless of count ("100k opaque can beat 20k layered transparent"): https://docs.idafaber3d.com/features/performance . Our 2-6k opaque clump target is therefore sensible for a PC MMO; make 2 LODs (full, ~40%).
- **Fitting to our head in Blender** (scriptable): (1) scale/position by matching the hair bounding box to the scalp vertex group; (2) add a Shrinkwrap on only the inner/scalp-contact verts (vertex group), `wrap_method='NEAREST_SURFACEPOINT'`, Snap Mode "Above Surface" (offset along the target's smooth normal), `offset` ~2-4 mm, target = body: https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/modeling/modifiers/deform/shrinkwrap.html , https://docs.blender.org/api/current/bpy.types.ShrinkwrapModifier.html ; (3) apply; (4) rig rigidly: create one vertex group named exactly `head` with all verts at weight 1.0, Armature modifier pointing at the body armature, no parenting to an Empty (Empties become spurious root bones on FBX export): https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/export-rigged-model-from-blender-to-ue4/9828 , https://nerivec.github.io/old-ue4-wiki/pages/exporting-the-mannequin-skeleton-from-unreal-engine-4-to-blender-and-re-importing.html . Long hair/beard tips can get 2-3 extra `hair_01..` bones later; everything else stays 100% head.
- **Brows/beard as texture masks vs meshes**: UE Material Layers let you stack a layer asset per feature (brows, stubble, beard) each with its own blend asset and instanced parameters; a grayscale mask texture sets per-pixel opacity of the layer: https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/using-material-layers-in-unreal-engine . Runtime swapping goes through a Dynamic Material Instance (`Create Dynamic Material Instance` -> `Set Material`) and texture-sample parameters exposed in the parent: https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/instanced-materials-in-unreal-engine , https://outscal.com/blog/dynamic-materials-unreal-engine . Caveat: an old thread reports layer/blend parameters not updating on MIDs when the instance wasn't the one assigned or the params weren't exposed; keep brow/beard masks as plain texture parameters on a flat (non-layered) face material if this bites: https://realtimevfx.com/t/can-you-change-dynamic-materials-with-material-layers-blends-at-runtime/19884 .

### Recommendation for ZombieMMO (hair/beard/brows)
1. Hair = opaque chunky clump meshes (2-6k tris, 2 LODs), ink separations painted into albedo + a baked normal map, outline from our existing comic post-process. No alpha cards.
2. Tripo prompt template: "standalone stylized <style> hair mesh only, no head/face/body, chunky sculpted clumps, low-poly volumetric, centered at origin, upright, discard any head". Expect to delete a scalp and remesh.
3. Script the fit: bbox-to-scalp scale, Shrinkwrap (Nearest Surface Point, Above Surface, 3 mm) on contact verts only, single `head` vertex group at 1.0, export with the body armature.
4. Brows and stubble/short beards = mask layers (R/G/B channels of one 1k mask per face, tinted by a colour parameter) swapped via MID texture parameters; only long/pointed beards get a small mesh rigged to `head` + `jaw`.

## 2. Fitting Tripo clothes to the base body in Blender (scripted)

### 2.1 Alignment and scale (bone-based)
- Read bone head positions from the body armature (`clavicle_l/r` for shoulder width, `pelvis`/`thigh_l` for hip height, `head` for stature) and compute the garment's required scale per axis from its bounding box; apply the transform before any modifier. No external source needed; keep the base body in A-pose/T-pose identical to the pose the Tripo model was generated in (prompt "A-pose" explicitly).

### 2.2 Shrinkwrap (tighten onto the body)
- Blender manual: `Nearest Surface Point` wrap method; Snap Mode `Above Surface` keeps verts above their floor faces with the offset applied along the target's smooth normal; `Offset` in Blender units: https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/modeling/modifiers/deform/shrinkwrap.html . API: `mod = ob.modifiers.new('fit','SHRINKWRAP'); mod.wrap_method='NEAREST_SURFACEPOINT'; mod.wrap_mode='ABOVE_SURFACE'; mod.offset=0.004; mod.target=body; mod.vertex_group='inner'`: https://docs.blender.org/api/current/bpy.types.ShrinkwrapModifier.html . Use 3-6 mm offset for tight clothes, 8-15 mm for jackets; only wrap a vertex group of the garment's inner/contact verts, never cuffs or hems, or the silhouette collapses: https://blenderartists.org/t/shrinkwrap-modifier/662206 .

### 2.3 Data Transfer of skin weights
- Manual: Data Transfer modifier, Vertex Data -> Vertex Groups, mapping `Nearest Face Interpolated` (nearest point on nearest source face, interpolated from that face's corners), Layers `All Layers`, and you must press **Generate Data Layers** (or create the groups) because the modifier does not create missing layers: https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/modeling/modifiers/modify/data_transfer.html . API: `mod=ob.modifiers.new('wt','DATA_TRANSFER'); mod.object=body; mod.use_vert_data=True; mod.data_types_verts={'VGROUP_WEIGHTS'}; mod.vert_mapping='POLYINTERP_NEAREST'; mod.layers_vgroup_select_src='ALL'; mod.layers_vgroup_select_dst='NAME'; bpy.ops.object.datalayout_transfer(modifier='wt'); bpy.ops.object.modifier_apply(modifier='wt')`: https://docs.blender.org/api/2.93/bpy.types.DataTransferModifier.html . Then `Limit Total` to 4 influences (UE default skin weight limit) and `Normalize All`.

### 2.4 Morph targets (body sliders) on clothes
- Forum consensus: UE will not transfer body morph deltas to a garment; the clothing shape keys have to be created in the DCC and driven with the same value in UE (`Set Morph Target` on both components): https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/morph-targets-for-additional-body-parts/56333 , https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/character-creation-body-customization-shape-keys-and-clothes-adjustment-how-to-best-practices/155194 . Scripted route: for each of the 51 body shape keys, set it to 1.0 on the body, add a temporary Surface Deform/Shrinkwrap pass or use the "Shape Transfer" add-on (Blender 3.6+): https://bartoszstyperek.gumroad.com/l/shape_transfer . Known pitfall: mismatched mid-value interpolation between garment and body can clip (belt vs pants example): https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/how-do-i-sync-morph-targets-shape-keys-when-i-need-one-to-ramp-up-faster/1603758 . UE 5.5 Mesh Deformer has no body-to-cloth morph copy that we could find **(unverified)**.

### 2.5 Export (Blender FBX) and import (UE)
- FBX: Armature tab `Add Leaf Bones` **off** (otherwise `_end` bones), `Only Deform Bones` on, Primary Y / Secondary X, Apply Scalings `FBX Units Scale`; leave `Apply Modifiers` off if shape keys must survive: https://app.cinevva.com/guides/blender-to-unreal-export-checklist , https://grifnmore.com/blender-to-ue-armature-fbx-export-steps/ . Garments export with the same armature object as the body so bone names/hierarchy match.
- UE import: pick the existing Skeleton asset; tick **Import Morph Targets** (Interchange, default in 5.5+, has it off by default): https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/fbx-import-options-reference-in-unreal-engine , https://protowlf.com/unrealcharactercompendium/skeletalmesh/ . Vertex colour import: Replace / Ignore / Override options; we need **Replace** so our HideMask vertex colours arrive: https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/interchange-import-reference-in-unreal-engine .

### 2.6 UE side: animating the parts and hiding the body
- Epic's comparison: **Leader Pose Component** = minimum setup cost, only the leader runs the AnimBP, but no physics on followers and a render-thread cost; **Copy Pose From Mesh** = medium cost, each follower runs its own AnimBP (allows RigidBody/AnimDynamics for capes/hair), leader must tick first (set as tick prerequisite); **Skeletal Mesh Merge** = one draw list but runtime merge cost: https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/working-with-modular-characters-in-unreal-engine , https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/copy-a-pose-in-unreal-engine . Morph targets on followers: Leader Pose shares curves with followers, so the same `Exp_*`/slider curves drive matching morphs on hair/clothes automatically.
- Hiding body under clothes, options in order of popularity: (a) separate body-part skeletal meshes toggled by booleans per garment ("coversTorso"): https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/do-video-games-hide-the-player-model-underneath-the-clothing/33335 ; (b) material mask (Masked blend + per-region mask params) which still renders the triangles: same thread, and for MetaHumans: https://yelzkizi.org/clothes-for-metahuman/ ; (c) `HideBoneByName` only scales bones to 0, hides all children and removes physics bodies, so it is unsuited to partial regions: https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/API/Runtime/Engine/USkinnedMeshComponent/HideBoneByName , https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/hide-unhide-a-specific-bone-by-name/481745 ; (d) Mutable removes hidden vertices at runtime: https://work.anticto.com/w/mutable/unreal-engine-4/user-documentation/remove-unseen-parts/ . Our vertex-colour HideMask is variant (b) with the region ID in vertex colour instead of a texture, which is cheaper than a texture lookup and keeps one body mesh; the only cost is that masked pixels still go through the vertex/pixel shader.

### 2.7 Tripo auto-rig / retopo / solidity
- Tripo API pricing: auto-rig 25 credits (rig check free), animation retarget 10 credits, retopology 10-30 credits; output rigs are animation-ready for UE: https://developers.tripo3d.ai/en , https://www.tripo3d.ai/features/ai-3d-model-generator . Tripo's rig is its own skeleton, not ours; use it only for previews, we always reskin with Data Transfer.
- No source states whether Tripo garments come hollow or solid **(unverified)**; in practice generated meshes are closed volumes (the generator produces watertight shells), so expect an inner shell. Tripo's cleanup guides: inspect in wireframe/X-ray for internal faces, then either Boolean-union or select inner faces -> Separate -> delete, then Delete Loose: https://www.tripo3d.ai/blog/explore/smart-mesh-mesh-cleanup-for-internal-faces-removal , https://www.tripo3d.ai/blog/how-to-clean-up-ai-generated-3d-models-in-blender . Scriptable heuristic: for each face, cast a ray along the normal; faces whose normal points toward the body centroid (dot < 0 with vector to nearest body surface point) are inner shell. Remesh/retopo rebuilds as a single shell and discards inner geometry but loses detail: https://blog.neural4d.com/user-guide/ai-3d-model-blender-cleanup-complete-mesh-fix-guide-2026/ .

### Recommendation for ZombieMMO (clothes)
1. One Blender script per garment: bone-based align -> inner-shell strip -> Shrinkwrap (Nearest Surface Point, Above Surface, 4-10 mm, contact group only) -> Data Transfer weights (Nearest Face Interpolated, All Layers, Generate Layers, Limit 4, Normalize) -> bake 51 slider shape keys via Shape Transfer -> FBX (no leaf bones, deform only).
2. UE: Leader Pose Component for everything (hair, clothes) now; switch a specific piece to Copy Pose only if it needs RigidBody physics.
3. Keep the vertex-colour HideMask on the body; store per-garment "covered regions" as a bitmask that the character BP pushes into the body MID.
4. Treat Tripo auto-rig as preview-only; never import its skeleton.

For voice chat use 5-6 shapes driven by amplitude/spectrum; precision is not the goal: https://mocaponline.com/blogs/mocap-news/lip-sync-animation-guide . ### 3.2 Blink, eye look-at, blending with sliders
- Blink: a timer-driven custom event in the AnimBP/character BP (random 2-6 s, 10% chance of double blink), driving a `Exp_BlinkL/R` curve 0->1 over ~80 ms, hold 40 ms, 1->0 over ~120 ms; forum example uses `Set Morph Target` with a 0.1 s delay: https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/issues-with-random-blink-setup-in-animbp-using-morph-targets-need-help-debugging/2413868 . Prefer feeding a named curve through `Modify Curve` in the AnimGraph so it blends with imported facial anims.
- Eye look-at: AnimGraph `Look At` skeletal control on `eye_l`/`eye_r`, `Look at Clamp` in degrees (only X/Y used when the look axis is Z), with interpolation time: https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/look-at?application_version=4.27 , https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/animation-blueprint-head-look-at-in-unreal-engine . Set the Up axis or the node rolls the bone: https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/how-to-use-look-at-node-in-animbp-without-rolling-the-bone/490178 . Clamp eyes to ~25 deg, head to ~pitch 30 / yaw 70 as in this thread: https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/ue-5-7-animbp-lookat-with-transform-modify-bone-head-neck-twist-wrong-direction-can-t-get-stable-fixated-look-at-target/2700861 .
- Blending sliders and expressions: both are morph targets driven by animation curves of the same name; all active morphs are summed additively, so there is no conflict as long as the 51 slider curves are held constant (set once from the character data via `Set Morph Target` or a `Modify Curve` node early in the graph) and the `Exp_*` curves come later from anim/lip-sync: https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/animation-curves-in-unreal-engine . Keep slider and expression deltas on disjoint-ish regions where possible (e.g. jaw-width slider vs `Exp_MouthOpen`) and let the jaw bone do the big opening so additive stacking doesn't over-stretch.

### Recommendation for ZombieMMO (talking/blinking)
1. Ship the amplitude-driven jaw + `Exp_MouthOpen` first (voice chat and any un-baked line), client-side only.
2. Bake NPC dialogue offline with Audio2Face-3D (MIT plugin, local NVIDIA GPU on the build machine) into Animation Sequences through an ARKit-named pose asset and the mapping above; replicate line IDs, never curves.
3. If real-time quality for custom rigs is needed later, buy Runtime MetaHuman Lip Sync (Standard model, custom pose asset) rather than reviving OVR Lip Sync.
4. Procedural blink + Look At (eyes 25 deg, head 30/70) in the AnimBP post-process; sliders set once via Modify Curve, Exp_* curves layered after; big jaw motion on the bone, not the morph.
